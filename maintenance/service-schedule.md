# Service Schedule - 2.2 TDCi

## Current Issues

### 1. Rear brake light out (one side)
- **Bulb type:** P21/4W (BAZ15D base, 21W/4W). Do NOT buy P21/5W (BAW15D / "380") - wrong pin offset
- **Cost:** ~2-3 EUR per bulb
- **Fuse:** F93 (20A) in passenger compartment - but single-side failure = bulb, not fuse
- **How to replace:** Open boot, remove side trim panel (3 clips), twist bulb holder anticlockwise, pull out, swap bulb, refit
- **Note:** Fix before ITV - a blown brake light is an automatic fail
- **Spanish:** Luz de freno / piloto de freno trasero

### 2. Parking sensor intermittent (temperature-dependent)
- **Symptom:** One rear sensor fails in cold/damp conditions, disabling the entire system
- **Likely cause:** Moisture in the sensor connector or micro-crack in the sensor housing (very common in Asturias climate)
- **Diagnosis:**
  1. On a cold morning when faulty, touch each rear sensor while in reverse - the dead one won't vibrate
  2. Unplug the suspect sensor connector, clean with contact cleaner, apply dielectric grease, reconnect
  3. If that doesn't fix it permanently, swap the suspect sensor with a known good one to confirm
- **Fix:** Clean connector + dielectric grease (free), or replace sensor (~15-30 EUR, push-fit into bumper)
- **PDC module location:** Boot, right-hand side behind trim (rarely the module itself at fault)
- **Note:** Non-functional parking sensors won't fail ITV, but the brake light will
- **Spanish:** Sensor de aparcamiento / sensor de parking

## Interval Summary

Ford originally recommended 30,000 km (18,000 mile) service intervals for the TDCi engines.
Many owners and independent mechanics recommend shorter intervals, especially for oil changes.
The schedule below uses **conservative intervals** appropriate for an older diesel.

## Regular Service (every 12 months or 15,000 km)

- [ ] Engine oil and filter change (5W-30, WSS-M2C913-C, ~6.2 L)
- [ ] Check all fluid levels (coolant, brake, power steering, washer)
- [ ] Check tyre pressures and condition (including spare)
- [ ] Visual inspection of brake pads and discs
- [ ] Check battery terminals for corrosion
- [ ] Check auxiliary drive belt condition
- [ ] Check for oil/fluid leaks underneath the car
- [ ] Check all lights and indicators
- [ ] Check windscreen wipers and washers
- [ ] Check handbrake operation and travel

## Major Service (every 24 months or 30,000 km)

Everything in the regular service, plus:

- [ ] Fuel filter replacement
- [ ] Air filter replacement
- [ ] Pollen/cabin filter replacement
- [ ] Brake fluid change (DOT 4, WSS-M6C65-A2)
- [ ] Clean or inspect EGR valve
- [ ] Inspect turbo hoses for cracks/leaks
- [ ] Check suspension components (bushes, drop links, ball joints)
- [ ] Check CV boot condition
- [ ] Check exhaust system for leaks/damage

## ITV (Inspeccion Tecnica de Vehiculos) - Spain

This car is Spanish-market. The ITV is Spain's roadworthiness inspection (equivalent to UK MOT).

### Inspection Frequency
- First 4 years: no inspection required
- 4 to 10 years old: every 2 years
- **Over 10 years old: annually** (this car is now over 10 years old, so annual ITV is required)

### Cost
- Typically 38-52 EUR for diesel vehicles (varies by autonomous community)

### Required Documents
- Ficha Tecnica (technical card)
- Permiso de Circulacion (registration document)
- Proof of insurance (recent receipt or certificate)
- DNI/NIE of the vehicle owner

### What They Check
- **VIN/chassis number** match with documents
- **Emissions** - diesel smoke opacity test (critical for older diesels)
- **Braking** - function, balance, and efficiency on all wheels
- **Suspension and steering** - shocks, tie rods, power steering leaks
- **Tyres** - tread depth (min 1.6 mm), wear pattern, correct sizing, no bulges
- **Lights and indicators** - all lights including fog lamps, brake lights, hazards
- **Xenon headlight alignment** - auto-levelling must work, beam aim is checked strictly
- **Body and structure** - rust, damage, mirrors, bumpers, windows
- **Seatbelts** - condition and function
- **Horn**
- **Exhaust** - condition and emissions

### ITV Tips for This Car
- Take the car for a 30+ minute motorway drive before the ITV to clear soot from the exhaust
- Ensure the EGR valve is clean - a dirty EGR increases smoke opacity
- Check all bulbs (including number plate lights) the day before
- Xenon auto-levelling must function correctly - this is checked specifically for xenon-equipped cars
- Clean the headlight lenses if hazy - poor light output is a fail point
- Ensure the parking sensors and ESP warning lights go off after startup (no stored fault lights)

## Seasonal Reminders (Asturias Climate)

Asturias has a wet Atlantic climate: frequent rain, mild temperatures, high humidity year-round,
occasional frost in winter, and moderate summers. Corrosion and moisture are the main enemies.

### Before Winter
- Check antifreeze concentration (should protect to at least -15°C for inland Asturias)
- Check battery condition - cold damp mornings are hard on diesel starting
- Test the Quickclear heated windscreen and heated mirrors (used daily in Asturias winters)
- Switch to winter washer fluid mix (frost is common, especially inland/mountain routes)
- Check tyre tread depth - rain and mountain roads demand good grip (min 3 mm recommended, not just legal 1.6 mm)
- Inspect wiper blades - they get heavy use in Asturias and wear faster
- Ensure all lights work (short days, frequent fog and low cloud)

### Before Summer
- Check A/C operation (regas every 2-3 years) - Asturias summers are mild but still humid
- Check coolant level and condition
- Inspect tyres for any damage from winter potholes (mountain roads can be rough)

### Year-Round (Atlantic/Asturias considerations)
- **Corrosion is the primary threat** - frequent rain plus proximity to the coast means constant moisture and salt air exposure
- Wash the underside regularly, especially after coastal drives or winter road salt
- Inspect the known Mk3 door sealant issue more frequently - Asturias humidity accelerates this
- Check drain holes in doors, sunroof channels, and boot area are clear (blocked drains = standing water = rust)
- The sunroof drain channels are particularly important - blockage leads to water in the headliner and footwells
- Cabin/pollen filter may need more frequent replacement due to high humidity and mould risk
- Keep an eye on brake discs - surface rust forms overnight in damp conditions (normal, clears after a few stops)

## High Mileage Notes (300,000 km)

At this mileage, the car is well into its extended service life. Several major items have already
been addressed (see maintenance history above), which is excellent.

### Still Due or Overdue
- [ ] Timing chain tension inspection (recommended at 150k km - overdue at 300k, the single most important outstanding check)
- [ ] Gearbox oil change (if never done - "lifetime fill" is optimistic at this mileage)
- [ ] Coolant system refresh (hoses, thermostat - rubber degrades. Water pump is done, but check the rest)
- [ ] Check remaining rubber bushes and mounts (at least one suspension bush replaced, check the rest + engine/gearbox mounts)

### Watch Closely
- **Turbo actuator:** If it has not failed yet at 300k, it is on borrowed time. Know the symptoms (see `common-problems.md`)
- **Injectors:** At 300k, injector seals and the injectors themselves may weep or fail. Listen for a ticking/knocking that changes with RPM
- **Wheel bearings:** Humming that changes with speed or turning indicates a worn bearing
- **CV joints:** Click when turning at low speed = worn outer CV joint. Inspect boots for splits
- **Steering rack:** Check for play and leaks from the rack seals
- **Turbo oil seals:** Blue/white smoke on startup or under boost = turbo oil seals leaking
