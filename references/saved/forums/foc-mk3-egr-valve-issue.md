**Gilgamesh** (March 26, 2018):
Disregard my post. 

Had the EGR out today and it was indeed caked with carbon. Blasted it with brake cleaner and gave it a proper scrub, and there has been a noticeable improvement. Much less smoke and soot on a limiter bounce. However, while the valve was out of the way I noticed a 6" split in the hose between the intercooler and EGR, which is definitely where my issue is. I have some power back, but not all. Either going to patch up the hose, replace it altogether or blank the valve completely. 

Found this video very helpful, and for anyone having the same problem I definitely recommend checking it out. I am by no means a qualified mechanic as I'm completely self-taught, and this video made the whole process very easy. Took less than half an hour to remove the valve, clean it and refit it. 

Note: My EGR does not have a sensor on it, so this may be present on some engines and not others. 

Hope this is helpful!

**Luke4efc** (March 26, 2018):
Don't blank the EGR is my advice at the minute unless you've got an "MOT friendly" garage. Under new testing rules coming in during May this year, any smoke emitted from a diesel vehicle is a fail. See point two here: https://www.gov.uk/government/news/mot-changes-20-may-2018

"Your vehicle will get a major fault if the MOT tester:

- can see smoke of any colour coming from the exhaust"

**stef123** (March 26, 2018):
Don’t bother with drilling a hole, that defeats the purpose of blanking it. If it’s an electronic type then yes the eml will come on here and there but I’ve noticed mine can also stay off for a good 6 months too lol. 

For me the reason behind blanking the egr was solely to stop it clogging up with carbon and casing issues. As I had previously without it blanked. 

Blanking the egr doesn’t cause any issues with mot at present but as of May it will be considered that you have tampered with an emissions system- given the egr and blank location it’s easy to spot when one is fitted.
